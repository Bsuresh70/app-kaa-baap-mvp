# App Kaa Baap — India-First Launch Playbook

**Positioning:** The Mother of All Apps  
**Launch market:** India  
**Initial product:** Mobile-friendly app discovery website/PWA  
**Available capital:** ₹15,000  
**Operating model:** Solo founder, bootstrapped  
**First 90-day goal:** Validate useful discovery and establish early user and developer participation.

## 1. Launch principle

Start with Indian apps and digital services, not an attempt to list every app in India. Prioritise quality, useful natural-language discovery, verified official links, and transparent listings.

App Kaa Baap is currently a discovery MVP. Do not claim universal sign-in, embedded access, live web-wide search, or third-party integrations until those capabilities are implemented and tested.

## 2. Initial categories

1. Government and citizen services
2. Education, exams and careers
3. Payments and everyday finance
4. Shopping, food and local commerce
5. Travel and mobility
6. Indian productivity, communication and AI tools

For every listing, record: name, category, concise use case, Indian origin/ownership or India relevance, supported platform, pricing (with verification date), country availability, official website, official app-store link where available, evidence/source, last checked date, and verification status.

Do not label a service “Indian-owned” unless that claim has been checked. Distinguish Indian-owned services, government services, and international services popular in India.

## 3. Differentiation

India already has app directories, including the Government of India's Gov.in AppStore for government apps. Do not position App Kaa Baap as the first or only app directory.

Differentiate through:
- Goal-first, natural-language discovery across multiple categories.
- Clear comparisons for user needs, price, language, platform and availability.
- A curated mix of verified Indian private-sector and public-sector services.
- Transparent explanations of why a result was recommended.
- A developer submission path and, later, integration capability.

## 4. First 14 days

### Product readiness
- Test natural-language Gemini search, keyword fallback, and country/price/platform filters.
- Check Netlify deployment and server-side API key configuration without exposing the key.
- Inspect duplicate discover function files and confirm which function Netlify deploys before removing or changing anything.
- Confirm all displayed links are official and open safely.
- Label the developer form as a demo until submissions are actually stored securely.
- Add a clear feedback method and basic privacy-conscious analytics.

### User validation
- Interview 15 potential users about how they currently discover apps and where they get stuck.
- Ask users to try real tasks, not merely comment on the idea.
- Recruit 10 developers for feedback on listing requirements and value proposition.
- Record failed searches and add catalogue entries based on demonstrated demand.

## 5. First 30-day targets

These are test targets, not guaranteed outcomes:
- 50 engaged users.
- 25 verified service listings.
- 15 user interviews.
- 10 developer conversations.
- 3 useful demonstration videos per week.
- 3 search-focused landing pages.
- A measured baseline for search-to-official-link clicks and returning users.

An engaged user completes a meaningful discovery session and opens a recommended official service. A verified listing has a checked identity and official destination.

## 6. Ninety-day distribution plan

### Days 1–15: Reliability
Fix product issues, verify catalogue entries, interview users and developers, and establish baseline analytics.

### Days 16–30: Small community launch
Publish three product demonstrations per week. Contact relevant college clubs, student groups, local startup communities and Indian app developers. Seek permission before promotional posts in communities.

### Days 31–60: Repeatable reach
Test community partnerships, shareable result pages, referral attribution and useful search landing pages. Offer free basic listings to early developers; do not promise traffic.

### Days 61–90: Scale only evidence
Repeat channels that bring engaged and returning users. Run paid experiments only after the discovery flow is reliable and there is evidence that users find results useful.

## 7. Capital plan — ₹15,000 available

Do not commit the full amount at launch. Use staged spending with a hard ceiling.

| Allocation | Maximum | Release rule |
|---|---:|---|
| Product readiness and essential tools | ₹2,000 | Spend only on a specific blocker; use existing free hosting/tools first |
| Content production and design | ₹2,000 | Prefer self-produced demonstrations and free tools |
| Community outreach and small demonstrations | ₹2,000 | Spend only for a confirmed, relevant activity |
| Paid acquisition experiments | ₹2,000 | Release only after product-readiness checks and organic signal |
| Uncommitted reserve | ₹7,000 | Preserve for unexpected needs; no routine spending |
| **Total capital** | **₹15,000** | |

Review spend weekly. Never pay for fake followers, bulk spam, unverified listings, or guaranteed-download schemes. Stop campaigns that create clicks but no meaningful discovery activity. Do not increase spending until cost per engaged user and return usage are measured.

## 8. Developer offer

Offer selected early participants a free basic listing during the pilot, with a clear profile, official links and the opportunity to shape listing standards. Describe analytics or integrations as future capabilities unless they are already working. Contact five relevant developers per working day with personalised messages.

## 9. Weekly scorecard

Track:
- Engaged users and returning users.
- Searches submitted and searches with useful results.
- Official-link click rate.
- Failed or empty searches.
- Verified listings and listings needing review.
- Developer responses and qualified partners.
- Referral source and content performance.
- Actual spend and cost per engaged user.

Review the numbers every Sunday. Improve the product before increasing promotion.

## 10. Safety and trust rules

- Keep Gemini/API keys server-side.
- Never collect third-party account passwords.
- Do not imply endorsement by a government body or app developer.
- Do not claim prices, ratings, security or availability are verified unless evidence supports it.
- Disclose sponsored placements if introduced later.
- Preserve user privacy and provide a way to report inaccurate listings.

## Immediate next action

Run a product-readiness test on the existing MVP and create a verified starter catalogue of Indian services. Only after these pass should the public launch and any paid promotion begin.
